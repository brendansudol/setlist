After the first two chords it is one move four times: D and A for two beats each, then a third chord. That chord is Bm twice, then G twice. The last G is cut to two beats so the bass can walk down the low E string, fret 3 to fret 2 to open, and land on Em. The second lesson and the chord chart give the same order and call the walk-down beat a D chord, which with that bass note is D over F#. The lesson counts everything from the first D out loud. It does not count the Bm and F#7 at the start, which are drawn here as a bar each.

Strumming

Neither this lesson nor the second one teaches a strumming pattern in words. The instructor counts beats and changes chords. What follows is what his picking hand does in two play-throughs, read from the video a frame at a time with the sound off. It shows the motion of the hand, not which strokes are meant to ring.

  • Where two notes in a row sit at the same fret on neighbouring strings, he rolls one fingertip across them instead of lifting it.
  • In the low turn he uses the ring finger at fret 7 and the index at fret 5, so the hand stays in one position.
  • Each slide is one finger: the ring finger in pass 1 and the middle finger in pass 4.
  • He corrects himself several times while talking the lick through, and says so. The slow play-throughs are the safer guide.

The whole song, in order

This is the order as the instructor describes it in part 2. He talks it through without playing the whole song and hesitates in places. The second lesson gives the same broad order. Neither was checked against the recording.

  1. Intro lick, four passes.
  2. Verse.
  3. Chorus. It ends on Em, and a short piece of the intro lick leads back to the top.
  4. Intro, verse and chorus again.
  5. Bridge, then a rest.
  6. Intro lick twice: once straight through, once stopping early.
  7. A solo over one pass of the verse chords. If you are not improvising, the instructor suggests strumming the verse chords or repeating the tune.
  8. Verse, as before.
  9. Chorus, this time with no bridge after it.
  10. Outro: the intro lick with extra notes on the end.

Three kinds of shape cover all eleven

  • Open chords. D, Am, G, C, A and Em. The lesson assumes you know these. Am is the one it never spells out.
  • Barre at fret 2. Bm, F#7 and F#m all keep the index finger across fret 2. F#7 and F#m are each the F major shape with one finger lifted: the pinky for F#7, the middle finger for F#m.
  • Three-note versions. The instructor's stand-ins for Bm and F#7 if the full barre is a struggle. It is one small hand shape, moved across one string.

Reading the diagrams: the left line is the low E string and the right line is the high E. The thick line at the top is the nut. Numbers are fingers, 1 for index through 4 for pinky, and a bar across several strings is one finger laid flat. × marks a string that is not part of the shape and a ring marks an open string. The letters underneath are the notes that sound. Frets and strings are the ones called out in the lesson and checked against the video; Am is read from the video alone. Finger numbers follow the lesson where it names a finger and the usual fingering otherwise.

What the lesson stresses

  • On the three-note shapes, sound only the strings you are holding. The instructor plays them both ways to show the difference.
  • Let the index finger do the muting, and keep it as relaxed as you can. He says the index is what stops the unused strings from ringing.
  • He often turns a minor chord into a minor seventh by lifting a finger, as with Em7 and F#m7. It is easier to hold and leaves a finger free.
  • He rates this an intermediate lesson and does not teach the open chords from scratch.

A practice order

  1. Loop the verse with the strumming above: D, Am, G, D, then D, C or Am, and a full bar of D. Count two beats a chord.
  2. Get Bm and F#7 clean on their own, as full barres or as the three-note versions, with nothing else ringing.
  3. Drill D to A to Bm: two beats, two beats, then a bar. Then the same with G in place of Bm.
  4. Add the end of the chorus: two beats of G, the walk down the low E string, and a bar of Em.
  5. Add the bridge: F#m, C and D, then F#7, C and D.
  6. Play the sections in the order listed above, with the intro lick wherever the list calls for it.
