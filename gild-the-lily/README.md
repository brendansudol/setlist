# Gild the Lily: how this page was made

**Live page:** https://brendansudol.github.io/setlist/gild-the-lily/

[`index.html`](index.html) is a one-file explainer for the rhythm-guitar part
of "Gild the Lily" by Billy Strings: eleven chord shapes, a beat-by-beat map
of the verse, chorus and bridge, and the order the sections come in. It was
worked out from a two-part YouTube lesson and is generated by
[`build.py`](build.py). Tapping any timestamp plays that moment of the lesson
in a YouTube player that stays on screen. This file records where the content
came from, how the page was produced, and what is and is not certain.

## Who made it

Claude (Anthropic's AI model) produced the page in the same working session
as the other three songs, using the same pipeline.

## The prompt

This page started as a question rather than a link:

> ok next up - i just learned the intro lick to billy string's gild the lilly
> song and now i want to learn how the rest (verses, chorus, etc.). is there a
> good youtube tutorial you would recommend for that? or another useful guide?

Claude searched YouTube, compared six lessons by length and chapter list, and
recommended this one because it is the only one that spends real time on
everything after the intro. It offered to make a page from it, and the reply
was:

> Build the page

That question shaped the page. The intro lick is the part already learned,
and it is a single-note line that the rules below would not let the page
write out anyway, so the page covers the chords and the song's order and only
points to the lick.

## Process

1. **Choosing the lesson.** Six candidates came out of YouTube search. Their
   lengths and chapter lists showed which ones stop at the intro.
2. **Facts about the videos.** Title, channel, length and publish date were
   read from `window.ytInitialPlayerResponse` on each watch page.
3. **Transcripts.** Both were read from YouTube's transcript panel: 50
   segments for part 1 and 92 for part 2. The instructor names every chord
   and, in part 2, spells most of them out string by string.
4. **Frames to confirm each shape.** Each of the eleven shapes was inspected
   at one or two moments, cropped to the fretting hand at 1080p. The chorus
   and bridge play-throughs were also sampled once a second, to check the
   order of the chords and roughly how long each one is held.
5. **A check against a chord chart.** One user-made chart
   ([GuitarTabsExplorer](https://www.guitartabsexplorer.com/strings-billy/gild-the-lily-chords))
   was compared with the lesson. What agreed and what did not is under "How
   sure each part is".
6. **Data, then a generator.** `build.py` started as a copy of the All The
   Small Things generator. The shapes and sections went in as data, and the
   script checks them before writing any HTML.
7. **Testing.** The page was served from a local web server and checked in a
   desktop browser at 1152 by 630 and 375 by 812: taps play the right moment
   of the right video, switching between the two parts keeps the chosen
   speed, the loop works, and nothing overflows on a phone. It was not tested
   on a real phone.

Three things were different from the earlier songs and are worth knowing for
next time. The background-tab technique itself is described in
[Your Song's write-up](../your-song/README.md#getting-frames-from-a-background-tab).

- **The transcript panel has changed.** Its segments are now
  `transcript-segment-view-model` elements inside an "In this video" panel.
  In a tab that is never shown, the panel only filled in after a screenshot
  forced the page to repaint.
- **Getting the text out.** The browser tool cuts a script's result at about
  a thousand characters, so each transcript was written to the console in
  pieces and read back from there.
- **Waking the video.** A muted audio element did not count as the tab having
  played media, and neither did a silent oscillator. Two seconds of a 20 Hz
  tone at one per cent volume did, after which the player's own
  `loadVideoById` call loaded the video.

The frames and the transcripts were working material only. Neither is in this
repository or on the page.

## How sure each part is

- **Confirmed by both the transcript and the frames:** which chords are in
  each section and in what order, the absence of a capo, and the frets and
  strings of ten of the eleven shapes.
- **Am comes from the frames alone.** The instructor names it and says he is
  not spelling out the open chords in part 1.
- **Tuning is inferred.** It is never stated. The frets he names only give
  these chords in standard tuning, and the page says exactly that.
- **Finger numbers.** He names the middle finger for the bass of G, one
  finger for A, and describes the two F# chords by which finger comes off
  the F major shape. The rest are the usual fingering.
- **The high E string on A.** He does not mention it, so the diagram leaves
  it out.
- **The three-note Bm** is B, A and D, which is strictly a Bm7. The card says
  so.
- **Beat counts in the verse** are his: two beats a chord, then a whole bar
  of D.
- **Beat counts in the chorus** are his from the first D onwards. He does not
  count the Bm and F#7 that open it. They are drawn as a bar each, and in his
  demonstration he holds the F#7 for longer than the Bm, so one of the two
  may be wrong.
- **The bridge is the least certain section.** He counts part of it aloud;
  the six-beat D and the four-beat C are read from frames a second apart. He
  introduces the section as F#m and C, and the second time through both the
  captions and the frames show F#7.
- **The order of the song** is his account, given without playing it through
  and with some hesitation. It was not checked against the recording.
- **Against the chord chart.** The chart lists the same nine chords in the
  key of D and gives the chorus in the same order, with one extra D before
  the final Em. It differs in two places: it has Am where this lesson's verse
  has C, and it gives the bridge as F#m, Am, D, then D, F#m, C, D. The page follows the lesson and says on the page
  that a chart disagrees. Nobody listened to the recording to settle it.

## Checks built into the generator

`build.py` refuses to write the page unless, for every shape:

- the notes produced by its strings and frets in standard tuning match the
  expected note list exactly;
- every note belongs to the chord the shape is named after;
- the lowest string in the shape gives the expected bass note;
- every fretted string has a finger, and no open or unused string does;
- any barre lies on strings in the shape and uses one finger;
- the shape fits in a four-fret diagram.

It also checks that every chord in a section lasts two beats or a bar, that
whole-bar chords start on beat 1, that each section ends on a bar line, that
every full chord is used in at least one section, and that all timestamps
fall inside the video they point to.

After the build, a separate pass confirmed that every in-page link has a
target and every timestamp link names the right video.

## Rules the page follows

The same rules as the other songs:

- **No video frames.** Diagrams are original drawings, and every shape links
  to its moment in the lesson.
- **No lyrics, and no note-by-note melody or TAB.** The intro lick, the solo
  and the outro lick are pointed to, not written out. The walk down at the
  end of the chorus is described in one sentence, as the instructor does.
- **Fingering is labelled honestly**, as described above.

## Design

Same layout, type and colour rule as the other songs. What is specific here:

- **The song map counts beats.** Each section is a row of boxes: a narrow box
  is two beats and a wide one is a whole bar. The other songs show one box
  per bar, which would hide the two-beat changes this song is built on.
- **No hand chart and no picking figure.** Every shape sits in the first four
  frets, and the lesson teaches chords, not a picking pattern.
- **Three families:** open chords, the three chords with a barre at fret 2,
  and the three-note stand-ins for two of those.
- **A list of the song's order,** because this is the first lesson here that
  covers a whole song.
- **Two videos.** Buttons say which part they play ("Part 2 at 3:10"), and
  the chapter list is split in two.

## The docked player

The same player as the other songs, described in the
[All The Small Things write-up](../all-the-small-things/README.md#the-docked-player).
This is the first lesson split across two videos, so the player's script
learned to switch: each timestamp link carries its video's id, and the player
loads that video if a different one is showing. The chosen speed carries
over and a loop is cleared. The same script went into all four pages so that
they stay identical.

- **33 timestamps play in the page:** eight from part 1 and twenty-five from
  part 2.
- **The shortcuts** beside the video on a wider window are Intro lick, Verse,
  Chorus, Bridge and Outro. They come from `PARTS` in `build.py`.

## Rebuilding

```bash
python3 gild-the-lily/build.py
```

Standard library only. Edit the data at the top of `build.py`, not
`index.html`; the next build overwrites the HTML.

## What is not in this repository

- The transcripts and the frames used to confirm the shapes.
- The intro lick, the solo and the outro lick. The lesson teaches the licks
  note by note; this page links to them.
