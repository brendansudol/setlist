The two grooves

The instructor's advice is to learn these before the riff, and to keep the playing hand moving and loose. Each slot below is an eighth note, counted “1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &”.

  • Thumb on the chord's bass note.
  • Down: the fingers flick down across the chord.
  • Up: the first finger brushes back up.
  • Hit: the playing hand lands on the strings. No chord sounds.
  • A riff note. The dots show when the notes fall, not which notes they are.

The riff

A guide to how the lesson teaches the riff: where on the neck it sits, when its notes fall and how the instructor fingers it. It is not a transcription. The lesson shows the riff in notation and tab on screen, and each button here puts that moment in the player.

  • The thumb plays every note of the riff. For the chord the instructor flicks down with the nail of his first finger, to get a crisper sound.
  • It moves by sliding. He starts with the third finger on the low E string and uses the first finger on the A string, and says Jack Johnson seems to play all of it with the first finger. Either is fine.
  • The first time the riff is sung over, and only then, a few of its notes change. The instructor admits he played that version all the way through for years.
  • In the chorus the riff bar starts with one bass note on beat 1, then the same six notes.
  • The last C of each verse has a riff of its own, in zone B. He says it outlines a C6 and that he often forgets it when he is singing.

The whole song, in order

  1. Intro: the riff into Am7, then into G7.
  2. The same four bars under the first line of singing, with the riff's one-off variation, then two D7 stabs.
  3. Verse 1. The last C gets its little riff.
  4. Chorus 1: groove, riff, groove, riff, ending on the D7 stabs.
  5. Verse 2, the same as verse 1.
  6. Chorus 2, the same but longer: it keeps bouncing between Am7 and G.
  7. Bridge.
  8. Verse 1 again.
  9. The long chorus again, with a little ad-libbing at the end.
  10. Finish on a G chord.

Three kinds of shape cover all ten

  • Barre with its root on the low E string. G, G7, Am7 and Bm7 are one hand shape with small changes. G7 is G with the little finger lifted, and Bm7 is Am7 two frets higher.
  • Barre with its root on the A string. C7, the big D7, Em and C. C7 and the big D7 are the same shape two frets apart. Em/D# is Em with its bass note moved.
  • Three-string grip. The small D7 used for the stabs and the verse.

Reading the diagrams: the left line is the low E string and the right line is the high E. No shape here is down at the nut, so every diagram has a label such as 5fr at its top left, which is the fret its top row stands for. What the lesson stresses

  • Stay relaxed. The instructor comes back to this more than anything else: the groove only sits right when the playing hand is loose and keeps moving.
  • The two grooves get their short sound in different ways. The verse is the fretting hand letting go; the chorus is the playing hand landing on the strings.
  • Learn the chorus groove before you add the riff to it.
  • It is fine to simplify at first: get the chords right and keep the strumming very plain.
  • If barre chords are still a struggle, he suggests this is not the song to start them on.

A practice order

  1. Get Am7 and G7 ringing on every string, one string at a time.
  2. Play the verse groove on one chord: thumb, flick, thumb, flick, pressing and relaxing the chord.
  3. Take it through the verse changes, two beats a chord, and add thumb, up, down, up on the C7.
  4. Play the chorus groove on Am7 alone, slowly: thumb, up, hit, up.
  5. Move it between Am7 and G, changing a touch early.
  6. Learn the riff from the lesson and drop it in between the groove bars.
  7. Add the bridge chords, then the alternating bass under them.
