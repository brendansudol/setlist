# Banana Pancakes: how this page was made

**Live page:** https://brendansudol.github.io/setlist/banana-pancakes/

[`index.html`](index.html) is a one-file explainer for Jack Johnson's "Banana
Pancakes": how the song is built bar by bar, the two grooves of the playing
hand, a guide to the riff, and ten chord shapes. It was worked out from a
YouTube guitar lesson and is generated by [`build.py`](build.py). Tapping any
timestamp plays that moment of the lesson in a YouTube player that stays on
screen. This file records where the content came from, how the page was
produced, and what is and is not certain.

## Who made it

Claude (Anthropic's AI model) produced the page in the same working session
as the other four songs.

## The prompt

> next up, let's do https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5-rVm6CdCaY&t=1155s -- if
> there are tabs in the video, you can reproduce and add them to the explainer
> page

The `t=1155s` in the link is 19:15, in the bridge, where the instructor adds
the alternating bass.

There are tabs in the video: the lesson shows standard notation and tablature
across the top of the screen for the riff and for each section. They are not
reproduced on this page. The tablature is the instructor's transcription of a
songwriter's music, and neither of them has given this site permission to
publish it, so a go-ahead from the site's owner does not settle it. Claude
does not copy it out, here or on the other songs.

What the page does instead:

- **It uses the on-screen notation as information.** Which chord falls in
  which bar, how long each lasts, which strings a chord uses and where the
  beats of each groove land were all read from it, and are drawn here in the
  page's own diagrams.
- **It puts the tab one tap away.** The docked player plays the lesson in the
  page, so tapping a section's button shows the lesson's own notation and tab
  for that section while the page's diagram stays next to it.
- **It maps the riff without writing it out.** The riff section shows which
  strings and frets the riff uses, when its notes fall, and how the
  instructor fingers it. It does not say which note comes when.

## Process

1. **Facts about the video.** Title, channel, length, publish date and the
   chapter list in the description were read from the watch page.
2. **Transcript.** YouTube's transcript panel gave 176 segments, of which 151
   are the lesson. The first three minutes are a performance of the song and
   were skipped; nothing sung is quoted anywhere here.
3. **The on-screen notation.** The video was sampled at about sixty moments
   to find every line of notation the lesson shows (bars 1 to 12 and 17 to
   36). Each line was then read at full resolution for its chord names, bar
   lines, rhythm and the strings each chord uses.
4. **Frames of the hand.** Four of the less common shapes were checked
   against the fretting hand, cropped at 1080p.
5. **Data, then a generator.** `build.py` started as a copy of the All The
   Small Things generator. The shapes, sections and grooves went in as data,
   and the script checks them before writing any HTML.
6. **Testing.** The page was served from a local web server and checked in a
   desktop browser at 1152 by 630 and 375 by 812: taps play the right moment,
   the shortcuts and speed button work, boxes in the song map land on the
   right shape or on the riff, and nothing overflows on a phone. It was not
   tested on a real phone.

The background-tab techniques (transcript panel, waking the video, reading
frames through a canvas) are described in the
[Your Song](../your-song/README.md#getting-frames-from-a-background-tab) and
[Gild the Lily](../gild-the-lily/README.md#process) write-ups.

The frames and the transcript were working material only. Neither is in this
repository or on the page.

## How sure each part is

- **Confirmed by both the transcript and the on-screen notation:** the chord
  names, the frets and strings of every shape, the two grooves, and the bar
  structure of the intro, verse, chorus and bridge.
- **Tuning is inferred.** It is never stated. The frets shown only give these
  chords in standard tuning, and the page says exactly that.
- **How many times the verse goes round** is not stated. The notation has a
  repeat sign but the count could not be read, so the page does not give one.
- **Bars 13 to 16 of the notation did not appear** at any of the moments
  sampled, so anything the lesson's score has there is missing. The chorus is
  described from bars 17 to 20 and from what the instructor says.
- **The first four bars of the bridge are played twice.** That comes from the
  instructor talking it through, not from a repeat sign.
- **Finger numbers.** He names the fingers for the small D7 and describes
  Am7, G7 and C7 in terms of the barre. The rest are the usual fingering.
- **The top string of Em/D#.** The notation keeps it at fret 7. The diagram
  leaves it out, because a frame of his hand shows the first finger reaching
  back to fret 6 on the A string, which makes that string hard to hold. That
  is a judgement from one frame.
- **G or G7.** He is fairly sure the record has G7 after the riff and says
  the verse is sometimes G7 too. The page follows what he plays: G7 after the
  riff, G elsewhere.
- **The order of the song** is his account at the end of the lesson. It was
  not checked against the recording.

## Checks built into the generator

`build.py` refuses to write the page unless, for every shape:

- the notes produced by its strings and frets in standard tuning match the
  expected note list exactly;
- every note belongs to the chord the shape is named after;
- the lowest string in the shape gives the expected bass note;
- every fretted string has a finger, and no open or unused string does;
- any barre lies on strings in the shape and uses one finger;
- the shape fits in a four-fret diagram.

It also checks that everything in a section lasts two beats or a bar, that
whole-bar items start on beat 1, that each section ends on a bar line, that
every shape is used in at least one section, that each groove has eight or
sixteen slots, that the riff's zones fit on the drawn neck, and that all
timestamps fall inside the video.

After the build, a separate pass confirmed that every in-page link has a
target.

## Rules the page follows

- **No video frames.** Diagrams are original drawings, and every shape links
  to its moment in the lesson.
- **No lyrics, and no note-by-note melody or TAB.** The riff is mapped and
  its rhythm is drawn, but its notes are not written out. See "The prompt"
  for why that holds even with permission from the site's owner.
- **Fingering is labelled honestly**, as described above.

## Design

The site's owner said, while the previous song was being made, that pages do
not all have to look the same and can be built around what a song needs. This
one is built around rhythm, because that is most of what the lesson teaches.

- **The song map counts beats** and has a box for the riff. A narrow box is
  two beats, a wide one a whole bar, and tapping a riff box jumps to the riff
  section.
- **Groove figures.** Each groove is a row of eighth-note slots with a symbol
  for what the playing hand does: thumb, down, up, or the percussive hit.
  There is one for the verse, one for the chorus, and one showing only when
  the riff's notes fall.
- **A map of the riff.** The first eight frets, drawn like tablature with the
  high E string on top, with two zones. It is in greys, because colour on
  these pages means a chord family and nothing else.
- **A bass table for the bridge** instead of another figure, since the groove
  is the same and only the thumb's strings change.
- **Three families:** barres with the root on the low E string, barres with
  the root on the A string, and the three-string D7. No shape is at the nut,
  so every diagram carries a fret label.

## The docked player

The same player as the other songs, described in the
[All The Small Things write-up](../all-the-small-things/README.md#the-docked-player).

- **45 timestamps play in the page.**
- **The shortcuts** beside the video on a wider window are Riff, Verse,
  Chorus groove, Bridge and Song order. They come from `PARTS` in `build.py`.
- **It matters more here than on the other pages,** because the lesson's
  notation and tab are on screen in the video. Playing a section in the
  docked player is how the page shows them.

## Rebuilding

```bash
python3 banana-pancakes/build.py
```

Standard library only. Edit the data at the top of `build.py`, not
`index.html`; the next build overwrites the HTML.

## What is not in this repository

- The transcript and the frames used to read the notation and check the
  shapes.
- The notes of the riff, and the lesson's notation and tablature.
