The Heart of Life: six chord shapes

Everything your fretting hand needs for the intro and verse, worked out from Six String Fingerpicking's 12-minute lesson on the John Mayer song. Tap any time on this page to play that moment of the lesson. The video stays on screen while you read.

Tuning
Standard in the lesson. The instructor notes the recording is tuned a half step lower.
Capo
None
To learn
6 chords built from 3 hand shapes
Loop
8 bars, used for both intro and verse

The eight-bar loop

  1. bar 1D
  2. bar 2D
  3. bar 3Bm
  4. bar 4Bm
  5. bar 5F#m
  6. bar 6G
  7. bar 7D/F#
  8. bar 8A

D and Bm last two bars each. The last four chords get one bar apiece, so those are the quick changes to drill. Tap a bar to jump to its shape.

Three hand shapes cover all six chords

  • Major shape, thumb over the top. D with the thumb on fret 10, G with the thumb on fret 3. One hand shape, moved seven frets.
  • Minor shape, thumb over the top. Bm at fret 7, F#m at fret 2. One hand shape, moved five frets.
  • Open chords. D/F# and A, down by the nut.

Reading the diagrams: the left line is the low E string and the right line is the high E. T is your thumb, wrapped over the top of the neck. Numbers are fingers, 1 for index through 4 for pinky. × marks a string the picking pattern skips, a ring marks an open string, and the letters underneath are the notes that sound. Frets and strings are the ones called out in the lesson; the finger numbers are the usual fingering for each shape.

How far the hand travels

Pinky notes for the second bar

D and Bm each last two bars. In the second bar the lesson adds one pinky note on the B string while the rest of the shape stays down.

D, second bar

Keep the whole D shape down and add your pinky on the B string at fret 12, two frets above the index.

Bm, second bar

Same idea, but the pinky lands on fret 10. That is three frets above the index, one more than on D.

Watch at 6:06

What the picking hand does on top

  • Thumb plucks the bass string
  • A finger plucks upward
  • Thumb taps the bass string, lightly
  • Index flicks down across the G string, together with the tap
  • The pattern only touches four strings: the bass string, D, G and B. That is why the diagrams mark the others as skipped.
  • The bass string is the low E for every chord except A, where the thumb moves to the open A string.
  • In the second bar of D and Bm the pattern thins out to pinch, tap, pinch, tap, with the pinky note in the second pinch.
  • The lesson finishes with a short second-guitar melody on the B and high E strings. That part is easier to watch than to read.

A practice order

  1. Hold the D shape at fret 10 and pluck the four strings one at a time until each rings.
  2. Slide that same hand down to fret 3 for G, then back. Repeat until you land without looking.
  3. Do the same with the minor shape: Bm at fret 7, F#m at fret 2.
  4. Loop the last four bars on their own: F#m, G, D/F#, A. These changes come fastest.
  5. Add the picking pattern on D alone, then run the whole loop slowly.
