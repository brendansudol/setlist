Four kinds of shape cover all fourteen

  • Open chords. D, A, A/C# and G, down by the capo.
  • The Bm barre shape. Bm at fret 2 and Em at fret 7 are one hand shape. Bm/A and Bm/G# keep that hand and change only the bass note.
  • Full barre at fret 2. F#m7 and F#7, one finger apart.
  • Small shapes over a bass note. G/D, A/D, Gmaj7 and G6 are a bass note plus two or three strings on top.

Reading the diagrams: the left line is the low E string and the right line is the high E. The thick line at the top is the capo; a label such as 7fr means the diagram starts higher up, at fret 7 above the capo. T is your thumb, wrapped over the top of the neck. Numbers are fingers, 1 for index through 4 for pinky. × marks a string that is not played in that shape and a ring marks an open string. The letters underneath are the note names as the lesson uses them, as if the capo were the nut; with the capo on fret 2 everything sounds a whole step higher. Frets and strings are the ones called out in the lesson and checked against the video. Finger numbers follow the video where it shows them clearly and the usual fingering otherwise.

How far the hand travels in the verse

Where the thumb finds the bass note

This arrangement has no single picking pattern to loop. Each chord gets its own short figure: the thumb plays the bass note and the fingers carry the tune on the upper strings. Those figures are the lesson itself, so this page does not write them out. What it can show is where each one starts.

  • The bass only ever uses the three low strings, and D always takes its bass from the open D string.
  • Bm, Bm/A and Bm/G# are one held shape. Practise that walking bass on its own before adding anything on top.
  • The second half of the verse keeps these bass notes and changes the rhythm in a few places. The video is the place to hear that.

Reaches worth drilling

A few single notes that the instructor singles out because the hand has to stretch for them while a shape stays down.

  • G/D in the intro

    With the shape held down, the pinky stretches up to fret 7 on the high E string.

    Watch at 3:32
  • A/D in the intro

    The arrangement reaches this chord by sliding the pinky along the G string from fret 4 to fret 6 rather than moving the whole shape. The instructor calls it a bit of a stretch.

    Watch at 2:37
  • A/C#

    The pinky hammers fret 5 on the high E string and pulls off. He says this one is awkward.

    Watch at 6:38
  • F#7

    The pinky slides from fret 5 to fret 7 on the high E string while the barre stays down.

    Watch at 11:55
  • D in line 2

    A stretch up to fret 5 on the high E string without letting go of D.

    Watch at 13:18

A practice order

  1. Play D, then G/D, then slide that small shape up two frets for A/D and back. Let the open D string ring the whole time.
  2. Hold Bm and change only the bass: A string fretted, A string open, then thumb on the low E at fret 4. Let go for G6.
  3. Hold the barre at fret 2 and switch between F#m7 and F#7 by adding and lifting the middle finger.
  4. Drill the one big move: D, up to Em at fret 7, back down to G.
  5. Run line 1 in order with the thumb only, one bass note per chord. Then line 2.
  6. Add the picking from the video, one chord at a time.
