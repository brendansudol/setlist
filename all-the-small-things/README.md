# All The Small Things: how this page was made

**Live page:** https://brendansudol.github.io/setlist/all-the-small-things/

[`index.html`](index.html) is a one-file explainer for the nine fretting-hand
shapes in the intro, first verse, pre-chorus and chorus of a fingerstyle
arrangement of "All The Small Things", worked out from a YouTube guitar
lesson. It is generated by [`build.py`](build.py). Tapping any timestamp plays
that moment of the lesson in a YouTube player that stays on screen: across the
top on a phone, in the right margin on a wide window. This file records where the content came from, how the page was produced, and
what is and is not certain.

| Tuning | Standard. The lesson does not say so; see "How sure each part is". |

## Who made it

Claude (Anthropic's AI model) produced the page in the same working session
as [Your Song](../your-song/README.md), using the same pipeline.

## The prompt

The whole request for this page was:

> now do the same for another youtube lesson: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hsBlzBTk9Xc

"The same" refers to the two earlier songs in this repository and the rules in
the handoff note quoted from in
[The Heart of Life's write-up](../heart-of-life/README.md).

The docked video player was added afterwards. The request was:

> can/should we add embedded video player so i can play the various parts
> right on this page? what about screenshots of various chord shapes? useful
> or no?

Claude recommended the player, advised against screenshots (the reasons are
under [The docked player](#the-docked-player)), and suggested trying it on one
page before the others. The reply was:

> One page first

This was that one page. The other two songs got the same player afterwards.

The first version put the video in a band across the top at every window
size. After trying it in a desktop browser, the next request was:

> can we iterate on the design -- i feel like we're wasting a lot of
> whitespace to left and right of player. put together a few different options

Claude built three alternatives behind a temporary switcher and compared them
at that window's size (1152 by 630). The reply was:

> Side rail

The options, and why this one, are under
[The docked player](#the-docked-player).

## Process

1. **Facts about the video.** Opened in Chrome. Title, channel, length,
   publish date and the chapter list in the description were read from
   `window.ytInitialPlayerResponse`.
2. **Transcript.** The "Show transcript" panel was opened and its 172
   segments read from the page. The instructor names every chord and most of
   the frets and strings out loud.
3. **Frames to confirm each shape.** The video was paused at about twenty
   moments across the four sections and the fretting hand inspected at 1080p. Fret
   wires were counted from the nut to read the fret, and the string was read
   from where the fingertip lands.
4. **Data, then a generator.** The shapes went into `build.py` as data. The
   script checks every shape before writing any HTML, then draws all the
   diagrams as inline SVG.
5. **Layout check.** The page was rendered at 390px wide in headless Chrome,
   in light and dark mode, section by section.
6. **The player, added later.** The page was served from a local web server
   and driven in a desktop browser at phone, small-phone, landscape and laptop
   sizes. Checked there: a tap plays the right moment, a second tap seeks, the
   speed and loop buttons work, closing and reopening keep the page where it
   was, and the timestamps fall back to plain links when the player is blocked
   from loading. After the layout change the same checks, without the blocked
   player, were repeated at 1152 by 630 (video in the margin), 900 by 700
   (controls beside the video) and 375 by 812 (phone). It was not tested on a
   real phone.

The frame-capture technique (playing a silent audio stream so a background
tab will load video, then drawing cropped frames onto a canvas in a labelled
grid) is described in
[Your Song's write-up](../your-song/README.md#getting-frames-from-a-background-tab).
It worked the same way here.

The frames and the transcript were working material only. Neither is in this
repository or on the page.

## What was captured

**Song map.** Every section is a four-bar unit.

| Section | Bars | Repeats | Video |

| Verse | C, G, Fmaj7, G | Four times (16 bars) | [4:44](https://youtu.be/hsBlzBTk9Xc?t=284) |
| Pre-chorus | C, C, G, F | Not stated | [11:46](https://youtu.be/hsBlzBTk9Xc?t=706) |
| Chorus | C, C, G, F | Twice | [13:43](https://youtu.be/hsBlzBTk9Xc?t=823) |

`x` is a string that is not part of the shape. `T` means the thumb frets that

**The lead-in chords.** The lesson calls the two quick chords before bar 1 G5
and F5. The frames show the same hand as the intro's G and F, so the page
treats them as those two shapes hit on the low strings only, and gives them no
cards of their own.

**The verse figure.** The lesson counts each verse bar "one, two and three,
four": the bass note, the D string, the G string, then two notes on the top
two strings. The page draws that outline and marks the last two notes as
changing from bar to bar. It does not say which notes they are.

## How sure each part is

- **Confirmed by both the transcript and the frames:** the song map, the
  absence of a capo, and the frets and strings of all nine shapes.
- **Tuning is inferred.** The instructor never states it. The frets he names
  (fret 3 on the A string for C, fret 3 on the low E string for G, fret 1 for
  F) only give those chords in standard tuning, and the page says exactly
  that.
- **Finger numbers.** The thumb on the two F shapes is spoken and visible.
  The intro's C, G and F match the usual barre and power-chord fingering in
  the frames. For the two one-finger G shapes the frames show which strings
  are fretted but not reliably which fingers, so those numbers are the usual
  fingering. The page states the general rule.
- **The top string on the verse G.** The diagram marks it as outside the
  shape, because the first verse bars do not play it. Later bars do use it
  open, and the card says so.
- **Pre-chorus repeats.** The lesson gives the four bars and plays them, but
  does not say how many times they repeat, so the page does not either.

## Checks built into the generator

`build.py` refuses to write the page unless, for every shape:

- the notes produced by its strings and frets in standard tuning match the
  expected note list exactly;
- every note belongs to the chord the shape is named after;
- the lowest string in the shape gives the expected bass note;
- every fretted string has a finger, and no open or unused string does;
- any barre lies on strings in the shape and uses one finger;
- the shape fits in a four-fret diagram.

It also checks that each section is four bars, that every shape is used in at
least one section, and that all timestamps fall inside the video.

After the build, a separate pass confirmed that every in-page link has a
target.

## Rules the page follows

The same rules as the other songs:

- **No video frames.** Diagrams are original drawings, and every shape links
  to its moment in the video.
- **No lyrics, and no note-by-note melody or TAB.** The verse figure is shown
  as an outline only, and the strummed sections are pointed to rather than
  written out.
- **Fingering is labelled honestly**, as described above.

One rule from the earlier songs was dropped here first. They had no embedded
player, because the pages were first made for a host that blocked iframes.
GitHub Pages does not, so this page got one, and the other two followed. The
page is still a single file and still contains no footage: the video is
played by YouTube's own player.

## Design

Same layout, type and colour rule as the other songs. What is specific here:

- **Three chord names, nine shapes.** Everything is a C, a G or an F, so the
  cards say where each version is used, and the song map links each bar to
  the right one.
- **Four families:** the power-chord shape (G, F), the C barre shape (C,
  Csus4), open shapes (open C and two one-finger Gs), and the thumb-over F
  (Fmaj7, F).
- **The hand chart is grouped by section**, which makes the main point
  visible: the intro is the only part played up the neck.
- **The picking grid is back**, as in The Heart of Life, because the verse has
  a repeating figure. Your Song had none.
- **The thick top line of a diagram is the nut.** There is no capo.

## The docked player

Every timestamp on the page is an ordinary link to YouTube. A script at the
bottom of the page turns a tap on one into "play that moment here".

All three songs have this player. Its styles, markup and script are the same
text in this `build.py`, in `your-song/build.py` and pasted into
`heart-of-life/index.html`; only the video, the shortcuts and the name of one
colour differ. A change to the player has to be made in all three.

- **One player, which stays on screen.** It appears on the first tap and stays
  put while the page scrolls, so the video and a diagram are visible together.
  Where it sits depends on the width of the window:

  | Window width | Where the video goes |
  |---|---|
  | Under 700px | A band across the top: the video, then a row of controls. The tapped card is scrolled out from under it. |
  | 700px to 1099px | The same band, with the name and controls beside the video, so the band is only as tall as the video. |
  | 1100px and up | A box in the right margin. The reading column slides left to make room and the page keeps the whole height of the window. |

- **In the margin, the video takes what the window has left** once the column
  has its full 760px: 340px at the least, 600px at the most (from about
  1,400px wide). The column only narrows in the tightest windows, so opening
  the video does not rewrap the cards.
- **Nothing is requested from YouTube until the first tap.** The player is
  then loaded with YouTube's IFrame Player API from `youtube-nocookie.com`.
- **Speed.** One button cycles 1×, 0.75× and 0.5×.
- **Loop.** One button, three presses: mark the start, mark the end (the loop
  begins), stop. Tapping another timestamp clears it.
- **Section shortcuts.** From 700px wide there is room beside or under the
  video for four buttons that jump to the intro, verse, pre-chorus and chorus.
  They were not asked for; they fill space the wider layouts free up.
- **Fallback.** Without JavaScript, when the file is opened straight from
  disk, or if the player fails to load, the timestamps stay plain links to
  YouTube.

Known limits:

- On a phone the first play may need a tap on the video itself, because
  mobile browsers often refuse to start sound from a script. This has not
  been checked on a real phone.
- YouTube may show ads in the player.
- A long name in the bar is cut short with an ellipsis on narrow screens.
- In a window just over 1100px wide the video in the margin is 340px wide,
  smaller than the band it replaces.

**Layouts that were tried.** At 1152 by 630 the first version, a band with
the controls under the video, took 267px of the height (42%) and left the
video with empty space on both sides. Three alternatives were built:

- *Side by side:* the controls beside the video. The band drops to 215px.
  Kept for windows too narrow for the margin.
- *Side rail:* the video in the right margin. No height lost. Chosen.
- *Corner:* a small floating video at the bottom right. Nothing moves, but it
  covers part of a card. Dropped.

**Why not screenshots as well.** With the player one tap away they add little:
a still of a hand is harder to read than a diagram, and the video shows hand
posture better in motion. They would also republish the instructor's footage,
where the player leaves it on YouTube.

## Rebuilding

```bash
python3 all-the-small-things/build.py
```

Standard library only. Edit the data at the top of `build.py`, not
`index.html`; the next build overwrites the HTML.

- The transcript and the frames used to confirm the shapes.
- The second verse and the bridge. They are not in this video.
