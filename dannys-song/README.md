# Danny's Song: how this page was made

**Live page:** https://brendansudol.github.io/setlist/dannys-song/

[`index.html`](index.html) is a one-file explainer for the guitar part to
"Danny's Song" by Loggins & Messina: a bar-by-bar map of the song, the Travis
pick used for its first half, the strum used for its second, and eight chord
shapes. It was worked out from a paid lesson and is generated by
[`build.py`](build.py). Unlike the other songs it has no video player. This
file records where the content came from, how the page was produced, and what
is and is not certain.

## Source

A lesson pack bought from [Jerry's Guitar Bar](https://www.jerrysguitarbar.com)
and kept on the site owner's own computer. None of its files are in this
repository or on the page.

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `01_dannys_song_playthru.mp4` | The arrangement played and sung, 4:20 |
| `02_dannys_song_part_1_lesson.mp4` | Part 1, 14:41: verses and choruses 1 and 2, Travis picked |
| `03_dannys_song_part_2_lesson.mp4` | Part 2, 10:58: verses and choruses 3 and 4, strummed, and the outro |
| `dannys_song_tab.pdf` | Four pages of notation and tablature for the whole arrangement |
| `dannys_song_songsheet.pdf` | The words with chord names above them |

|---|---|

| Tuning | Standard. Not stated; the shapes are the ordinary open chords. |
| Capo | None. The nut is clear in all three videos. |

## Who made it

Claude (Anthropic's AI model) produced the page in the same working session
as the other five songs.

## The prompt

> okay, next up - take a look at lm_dannys_song_zsna2 folder i have locally in
> Documents/guitar/ i think and let's do that one

Two things about this source needed a decision from the site's owner before
any work started, and Claude asked:

- **How times should work.** The videos are local files, so the page cannot
  play them the way the other pages play YouTube. The choice was between
  letting the reader load their own copies into the page's player, and plain
  written-out times. The answer was plain times.
- **Whether to download a speech model.** Hearing what the instructor says
  meant transcribing the audio on the owner's computer, which needed a
  one-time download of OpenAI's Whisper model. The answer was yes, the
  `small.en` model.

## Process

1. **The two PDFs.** The chord sheet gave the chord names and the order of
   verses and choruses. The tab gave the bar structure, the tempo, which
   strings each chord uses and the section labels. Neither is reproduced.
2. **Transcripts.** The audio of the two lesson videos was extracted with
   macOS's `afconvert` and transcribed on the computer with Whisper
   (`small.en`): 116 segments for part 1 and 77 for part 2. The audio was not
   sent anywhere. The instructor calls out every string he picks by number,
   so the transcripts carry most of the lesson.
3. **Frames.** About forty frames were pulled from the three videos to check
   for a capo, see how the videos are laid out and look at a few shapes. The
   videos are 640 by 360, so the frames settle little about fingering.
4. **Data, then a generator.** `build.py` started as a copy of the Banana
   Pancakes generator with the video player taken out. The shapes and
   sections went in as data, and the script checks them before writing any
   HTML.
5. **Layout check.** The page was rendered at 900px wide in headless Chrome
   and at 375 by 812 in a desktop browser, and checked for overflow.

Two tools were not what the earlier songs used. `ffmpeg` was installed but
would not start on this computer, so audio went through `afconvert`, and
frames came from a short Swift script using AVFoundation.

The transcripts and the frames were working material only. Neither is in
this repository or on the page.

## What was captured

**Sections.** Four beats to the bar. A chord with a number after it lasts that
many beats.

| Section | Chords | Lesson |
|---|---|---|
| Intro | D 4 | Part 1, 0:11 |
| Verse | D 2, C 2, Bm 4, E 4, A 4. Twice. | Part 1, 1:00 |
| Chorus | G 2, A 2, D 1, D/C# 1, Bm 1, D/A 1, G 2, A 2, D 1, D/C# 1, Bm 2, G 2, A 2, D 2, C 2, Bm 2, E 2, A 4 | Part 1, 6:49 |
| Outro | D 2, C 2, Bm 4, E 2, A 2, D 4 | Part 2, 9:04 |

**The order of the song:** intro; verse and chorus twice, picked; verse and
chorus twice more, strummed; the four verse bars once with no singing; outro.

**The Travis pick.** On D the thumb alternates between the D and G strings in
eighth notes while the index and middle fingers play the B and high E strings
in the gaps: strings 4, 2, 3, 1, four times a bar. On the other chords the
thumb starts from the chord's root and the fingers shift across.

**The strum**, in sixteenth notes, as two shapes the instructor treats as
interchangeable:

| Shape | Two beats | Lesson |
|---|---|---|
| Sparser | Thumb, down, thumb, a picked note or an up, down, up | Part 2, 0:32 |
| Busier | Thumb, down, up, thumb, a picked note or an up, down, up | Part 2, 1:43 |

**Shapes.** Frets are listed from string 6 (low E) to string 1 (high E). `x`
is a string that is not part of the shape.

## How sure each part is

- **Confirmed by the tab and by what the instructor says:** the chord names,
  the bar structure of the verse, chorus and outro, the picking pattern on D,
  the two strum shapes, and which strings the thumb uses on each chord.
- **The tab and the chord sheet disagree about the outro.** The sheet gives E
  and A a bar each; the tab puts them in one bar, and the instructor says E
  only lasts half a bar there. The page follows the tab.
- **Tuning is inferred.** It is never stated.
- **Finger numbers are the usual fingering throughout.** The instructor talks
  about the picking hand in detail and says almost nothing about which
  fretting finger goes where. The videos are too small to read it from.
- **D/C#.** The frets come from the lesson. The finger shown on the bass note
  is a guess, and the instructor says the top string can be left open to make
  the reach easier.
- **G.** The picked version in the tab does not use the A string; the
  strummed version hammers onto it at fret 2. The diagram shows the full
  open G.
- **The transcripts are machine-made.** String numbers and chord names were
  cross-checked against the tab, but a time on the page could be a few
  seconds out.
- **The order of the song** comes from the chord sheet and from the
  instructor's account of the ending in part 2. It was not checked against
  the recording.

## Checks built into the generator

`build.py` refuses to write the page unless, for every shape:

- the notes produced by its strings and frets in standard tuning match the
  expected note list exactly;
- every note belongs to the chord the shape is named after;
- the lowest string in the shape gives the expected bass note;
- every fretted string has a finger, and no open or unused string does;
- any barre lies on strings in the shape and uses one finger;
- the shape fits in a four-fret diagram.

It also checks that every chord in a section lasts one beat, two beats or a
bar and starts where such a chord can, that each section ends on a bar line,
that every shape is used in at least one section, that each strumming bar has
sixteen slots, and that every time falls inside the video it names.

After the build, a separate pass confirmed that every in-page link has a
target.

## Rules the page follows

- **None of the lesson's files are published.** Not the videos, not frames
  from them, not the tab and not the chord sheet. The lesson is something its
  maker sells.
- **No lyrics, and no note-by-note tablature.** The picking pattern is shown
  as a pattern: which string, which finger, in what order, on one chord. What
  the lesson's tab adds bar by bar is described in words and pointed to by
  time.
- **Fingering is labelled honestly**, as described above.

## Design

Built around what this lesson teaches, which is the picking hand.

- **No video player.** Every time is a small label, such as "Part 1 at 4:43",
  that is not a link. A list near the top says which file each label means.
- **The song map counts beats.** A narrow box is two beats and a wide one a
  whole bar. Two chords that last a beat each share a narrow box, which jumps
  to the less familiar of the two.
- **A picking grid.** One bar on D, with a row for each string and a column
  for each sixteenth note, marking the thumb and the two fingers.
- **Two strum figures** in the same sixteenth-note columns, and a table of
  where the thumb goes on each chord.
- **Three families:** open chords, the one barre chord, and D over a moving
  bass note.

## Rebuilding

```bash
python3 dannys-song/build.py
```

Standard library only. Edit the data at the top of `build.py`, not
`index.html`; the next build overwrites the HTML.
