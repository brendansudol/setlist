Danny's Song: a Travis pick, a strum, eight shapes

The guitar part to the Loggins & Messina song as Jerry's Guitar Bar teaches it: Travis picking for the first two verses and choruses, an optional strummed version for the last two, and the chords under both. The lesson is a paid download, so this page cannot play it. Every time is written out for you to find in your own copy.

How the song is built

Four beats to the bar. A narrow box is two beats and a wide box is a whole bar. A box with two names has two chords, one beat each. Tap a box to jump to its shape; a box with two names goes to the less familiar one.

The whole song, in order

  1. Intro.
  2. Verse 1 and chorus 1, picked.
  3. Verse 2 and chorus 2, picked the same way.
  4. Verse 3 and chorus 3, strummed.
  5. Verse 4 and chorus 4, strummed the same way.
  6. The four verse bars once more, with no singing.
  7. Outro.

The strummed half is the instructor's own idea, for variety. He says the guitar on the record keeps picking all the way through while other instruments join in, and that playing all four verses the picked way is just as good.

The Travis pick

The first half of the song is one small pattern, kept going while the chords change under it. The thumb plays two strings in turn, steadily, and a finger plays in each gap.

  • Get the thumb even first. The instructor wants it to go like a metronome.
  • D only has four strings, so the thumb takes the D and G strings and the fingers take the top two. On every other chord the thumb drops down a string or two and the fingers move over to follow it.
  • The thumb's first string is the chord's root: the A string for C, Bm and A, the low E string for E and G.
  • Changes come a touch early. The last note before a new chord is often an open string, and he tends to land on D before its bar begins.
  • Some bars open with the bass note and a light brush down with the backs of the fingernails in place of the first two picked notes. It happens on D in the second line of the verse, and on G and D in the chorus.
  • In the third bar of the chorus the thumb swaps its second string, going to the G string on both G and A.
  • The top string picked is not always the same from one pass to the next. The instructor says it does not matter: any pattern you have already played on those chords will work.

The strum

From verse 3 the chords stay the same and only the playing hand changes: the thumb still plays bass notes, and the fingers brush the strings instead of picking them. The lesson uses two shapes and treats them as interchangeable. The instructor's words are not to worry about exactly what the tab says on any of these bars.

  • Thumb on one of the chord's two bass strings.
  • Down: a light brush with the backs of the fingernails.
  • Up: a flick with the index finger that catches the top string or two.
  • A picked note on the G string. The instructor says an up strum does just as well.

Three kinds of shape cover all eight

  • Open chords. D, C, E, A and G. The lesson assumes you know them.
  • Barre chord. Bm, at fret 2.
  • D over a moving bass. D/C# and D/A are the D chord with a different string under the thumb. They only last a beat each.

Reading the diagrams: the left line is the low E string and the right line is the high E. The thick line at the top is the nut. Numbers are fingers, 1 for index through 4 for pinky, and a bar across several strings is one finger laid flat. × marks a string that is not part of the shape and a ring marks an open string. The letters underneath are the notes that sound. Frets and strings come from the lesson's tab and what the instructor says. He does not say which finger goes where on these chords, so the finger numbers are the usual fingering.

A practice order

  1. Hold D and play the thumb alone, D string then G string, until it is even.
  2. Add the fingers in the gaps: B string, then high E string. That bar is the intro.
  3. Take the pattern through the verse chords, letting the thumb move to each chord's root.
  4. Learn the walk down in the chorus slowly: D, D/C#, Bm, D/A, one beat each.
  5. Follow part 1 bar by bar for the small changes, then play verse and chorus with the play-through.
  6. If you want the strummed half, start with thumb and a brush down on each chord, then add the rest.
